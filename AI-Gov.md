# AI Ethics and Legal Responsibility

## Course Objectives
1.	Understand artificial intelligence as a legal, ethical, social, economic, and governance challenge.
2.	Examine how AI systems affect human agency, autonomy, equality, privacy, accountability, markets, public institutions, and democratic participation.
3.	Introduce students to major AI governance approaches, including human-centred AI, rights-based governance, risk-based regulation, audits, impact assessments, and responsible innovation.
4.	Analyse key legal and policy concerns relating to bias, discrimination, surveillance, misinformation, platform power, data governance, and institutional accountability.
5.	Develop the ability to assess AI-related risks and propose ethically sound, legally responsible, and policy-sensitive governance responses.

## Learning Outcomes
By the end of the course, students will be able to:
1.	Explain the key ethical, legal, and governance challenges posed by artificial intelligence systems, including bias, opacity, surveillance, misinformation, accountability, and institutional responsibility. 
2.	Analyse how AI systems affect human agency, autonomy, equality, privacy, markets, platforms, and public decision-making. 
3.	Evaluate emerging AI governance models, including rights-based regulation, risk-based regulation, human-centred AI, self-regulation, co-regulation, and accountability-based approaches. 
4.	Apply basic AI risk-assessment and policy-design tools to propose legally responsible and ethically sound governance responses for selected AI use cases.

## Course Modules
### Module 1: Introduction to AI Ethics, Governance and Legal Responsibility
-	Intelligence, Natural Intelligence, and Artificial Intelligence
-	AI as a socio-technical system 
-	Why AI raises questions of ethics, law, power, markets, and institutional accountability
##### Recommended Readings:
- Kate Crawford, Atlas of AI: Power, Politics, and the Planetary Costs of Artificial Intelligence.
- Brent Mittelstadt, Principles Alone Cannot Guarantee Ethical AI, Nature Machine Intelligence 1, 501–507, 2019. https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-019-0114-4

### Module 2: Human Agency, Autonomy and Algorithmic Power
-	Agency, Autonomy, and the Emergence of Private Ordering
-	AI-mediated choices, nudging, manipulation, recommender systems, dark patterns, behavioural profiling, and loss of meaningful human control
-	The problem with centralised choice engineering
##### Recommended Readings:
- Daniel Susser, Beate Roessler & Helen Nissenbaum, Technology, Autonomy, and Manipulation, Internet Policy Review 8(2), 2019. https://doi.org/10.14763/2019.2.1410
- Shoshana Zuboff, The Age of Surveillance Capitalism.
- Karen Yeung, ‘Hypernudge’: Big Data as a Mode of Regulation by Design, Information, Communication & Society 20(1), 2017. https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118X.2016.1186713
- Julie E. Cohen, Between Truth and Power: The Legal Constructions of Informational Capitalism.

### Module 3: Principles and Models of AI Governance
-	Rights-based regulation vs. Risk-based regulation, regulatory errors
-	Regulation, co-regulation, and self-regulation
-	Human-centred AI

### Module 4: Bias, Discrimination, Fairness and Social Exclusion
-	Algorithmic bias 
-	equality, due process, and structural discrimination
-	AI optimism and socio-economic status
##### Recommended Readings:
- Safiya Umoja Noble, Algorithms of Oppression: How Search Engines Reinforce Racism.
- Ruha Benjamin, Race After Technology: Abolitionist Tools for the New Jim Code.

### Module 5: Privacy, Data Governance, Surveillance and Consent
-	Consent, Contract, and Privacy
-	Data extraction and critical data studies
##### Recommended Readings:
- Helen Nissenbaum, Privacy in Context: Technology, Policy, and the Integrity of Social Life.
- Julie E. Cohen, Configuring the Networked Self.

### Module 6: AI, Markets, Platforms and the Law
-	Platform power, data dominance, and competition policy
-	Consumer protection in AI-driven environments 
##### Recommended Readings:
- Petter Törnberg & Justus Uitermark, Seeing like a platform (2025). OA version at https://doi.org/10.4324/9781003326861

### Module 7: Generative AI, Misinformation and Public Responsibility
-	Deepfakes, synthetic media, and mis-information vs. dis-information 
-	Academic integrity, copyright concerns, professional misuse, and responsibility for AI-generated content
##### Recommended Readings:
- Katherine Lee, A. Feder Cooper & James Grimmelmann, Talkin’ ’Bout AI Generation: Copyright and the Generative-AI Supply Chain. https://www.law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Talkin-Bout-AI-Generation-1.pdf

### Module 8: AI Risk Assessment, Accountability and Institutional Design
-	Contract law as the mechanism of risk allocation
-	Explainable AI, human oversight, and transparency
-	Institutional design for effective governance
##### Recommended Readings:
- Anshuman Sahoo & Arindam Basu, Contracting with Artificial Intelligence. https://doi.org/10.54648/erpl2025068
- EU, Liability Rules for Artificial Intelligence. https://commission.europa.eu/topics/business-and-industry/doing-business-eu/contract-rules/digital-contracts/liability-rules-artificial-intelligence_en
